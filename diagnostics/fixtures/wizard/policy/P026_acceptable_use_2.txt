ATLAS POLICY — P009-2
Subject: Acceptable use and prohibited abuse
Current edition: 2026.09
Status: Current
Applies to: Atlas hosted service unless a signed agreement states otherwise.

This fictional policy corpus is designed for RAG evaluation. The rules are intentionally specific and include exceptions, precedence, timing, and plan distinctions so an answer must retrieve the correct controlling material rather than rely on surface similarity.

1. Controlling rules

The service may not be used to distribute malware, conduct credential theft, facilitate non-consensual surveillance, or intentionally evade access controls.

Customers may not use automated messaging to deceive recipients about the identity of the sender.

Testing security controls is permitted only on resources the customer owns or is explicitly authorized to test.

2. Precedence and interpretation

Policy precedence:
1. A signed Enterprise order form controls an explicitly negotiated commercial term.
2. A Data Processing Addendum controls conflicting privacy or data-processing obligations.
3. A Security Addendum controls conflicting security commitments.
4. The current policy edition controls over superseded policy editions.
5. Operational help-center guidance explains implementation but does not override a controlling policy or signed agreement.

An override is narrow. Changing one contracted limit does not silently replace unrelated retention, security, or acceptable-use rules.

3. Plan and region context

For the Business plan, the default retention period is 365 days, the API allowance is 1,200 requests per minute, and the attachment limit is 100 MB. These product defaults are not themselves refund rights or SLA commitments.

For the Starter plan, the corresponding defaults are 30 days, 300 requests per minute, and 10 MB. Support staff must not mix entitlements between the two plans.

For a workspace whose content region is Canada, customer content is pinned to the Canadian data plane when the workspace region is Canada. The location of limited billing or security metadata does not change the workspace's selected customer-content region.

4. Exceptions and non-exceptions

An exception must come from a controlling source. A support ticket, sales chat, old screenshot, or outdated help article does not by itself amend the current policy. A signed order form can alter an explicitly negotiated term, but silence is not an override.

Mandatory law can create rights beyond this policy. When that occurs, support should escalate rather than inventing a universal rule for every customer. Operational inconvenience is not automatically an exception.

5. Enforcement and evidence

When a policy action depends on timing, use the authoritative service timestamp rather than a user's local clock screenshot. When it depends on usage, use the service's metered usage record. When it depends on an account role, use effective role membership at the time of the action.

The service should retain safe identifiers, timestamps, decision codes, and audit evidence necessary to explain a decision without copying unrelated private content into diagnostics.

6. Historical clarification

Before the 2026.09 edition, some documentation used shorter descriptions that omitted edge cases. The current edition does not automatically change a signed agreement, but it supersedes prior general-policy wording for customers governed by the standard terms. Historical examples are included for interpretation only.
